What the discovery turned up

Findings that changed the plan, not just filled it in.

The team bios were nearly lost. All 12 bios live on /team-blocks — a NOINDEX page absent from the sitemap, reachable only via popup anchors. A sitemap-driven harvest drops 1,341 words covering every person in the firm and nobody notices until the client asks. Recovered.
Those bios are invisible to Google today. Because they sit on a NOINDEX page, none of that content ranks. Moving it inline on /our-team makes it crawlable for the first time — the single biggest free SEO gain in the project.
33 of 35 URLs keep their exact path. Same domain, same slugs, www stays canonical. Only /home → / and /team-blocks → /our-team need 301s. This is about as low-risk as a migration gets — provided nobody "tidies" a slug.
The site has no analytics at all. No GA4, no GTM, no pixel — only Squarespace's built-in, which doesn't survive. Being provisioned fresh. Historical data must be exported before the account lapses or the firm's entire baseline is gone.
The new design fixed two problems at once. The old palette had no accent colour and one pairing failed WCAG at ~1.3:1; every pairing in the new one passes AA. And the old body font was Adobe Fonts, which can't be self-hosted under our CSP — the new faces are open-licence. Both blockers closed by the design rather than worked around.
SEO parity is green — 0 regressions across all 34 old URLs. 32 answer 200 direct at exactly the old path; only the two mapped redirects fire. 10 pages gained the meta description the old site never had. Two bugs surfaced only because we diffed against a captured baseline rather than eyeballing pages: a description shipping &amp; into search results, and the parity checker itself reading the wrong field so every page scored a false pass.
Accessibility is 100, after a correction. Phase 4 reported every colour pairing passing WCAG. That measured the design tokens — but the design mutes text with opacity, which lowers contrast as rendered. Six places were below AA, worst at 3.27:1. Replaced with an explicit dim colour measured on every background. Check contrast as rendered, not as specified.
The English site is built and live on staging. 35 pages at ghs.coreshift.page, Live Edit auditor READY with every page scanned. All 35 old URLs verified 200 direct against the deployment — only the two mapped redirects fire. Images cut 12.3 MB to 2.2 MB; fonts, GSAP and the widget self-hosted so the CSP stays default-src 'self'. The Chinese tree can't be built until translated copy exists.
Scope changed: the site is going bilingual. The approved design shipped an EN/中文 toggle that the original scope excluded. Decision taken to build it: 36 pages become 72 and ~23,460 words need a human legal translator. Recorded as an explicit amendment to the scope lock, not absorbed quietly. Translation is now the critical path — the build can be finished and still not launchable.
Bilingual costs the URL map nothing. Chinese lives under /zh/ as 36 brand-new URLs, so the English paths never move and the migration stays a two-redirect job. Relocating English to /en/ would have thrown that away — explicitly ruled out. One subtlety: page content needs no editing-key changes because overrides are stored per path, but navigation is shared site-wide, so nav keys are locale-scoped or editing the English menu silently rewrites the Chinese one.
Four blog images look unlicensed. Filenames indicate a Getty asset and images pulled off news sites and Google Images. Quarantined rather than migrated — republishing them on a law firm's own site isn't a call to make silently. Default is to replace with licensed stock.
