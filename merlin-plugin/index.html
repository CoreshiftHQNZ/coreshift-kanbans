P6TASK-08112 Aug

A learner still needs Node, and the fix is gated off

Found for real on install week, not in planning: .mcp.json and hooks.json both spawn node in exec form, so neither goes through a shell and both need the system PATH. Merlin greeted a brand-new person with somebody else’s work, by name

P6TASK-0747 Aug

Nothing Merlin recorded was reaching us, and every test said it was

On the one machine that had been running for a week, every attempt to send was timing out. The work was arriving — hundreds of records stored correctly — but the reply never came back in time, so Merlin never learned it had succeeded, never cleared its queue, and sent a bigger batch next time. A backlog that could only grow, on records that can never be edited or deleted. Measured rather than guessed at, which settled three suspects in one run: one item took three seconds, ten took seven, fifty took twenty-five, a hundred and thirty-seven took a minute. A rejected request came back in under a second, which ruled out the server being slow to wake, the new rate limit and the background hooks without touching any of them. The cause was that items were being saved one at a time, each a separate trip to the database, against a client that waits five seconds — two numbers living in different files that nothing had ever compared. Beyond about four items it could not succeed. Now the whole batch is saved in one go: two hundred items cost less than one used to. Why eight hundred tests never saw it is the part worth keeping: every test sends a handful and clears immediately, and at one or two items the old way fitted inside the limit. The failure needed a backlog, and nothing built one. Evidence about small batches was being read as evidence about the whole path. The new test measures the two numbers against each other, so changing either re-checks the pair. Verified on the real machine: the queue went from a hundred and forty-four to empty in one go.

P6TASK-0716 Aug

Nobody could actually start using Merlin, and it took asking for the install instructions to find out

Asked to write the instructions a new person would follow, there was nothing to instruct. Three of the four reasons were mechanical — no package, nothing on the main branch, the shared catalogue pointing at a version that did not contain the product. The fourth was a hole in the design that nobody had named: there was no way for a person to be recognised as themselves. Merlin identifies each learner from a small credential issued by our servers, and nothing anywhere put that credential onto their machine. The only two routes were editing a settings file or hand-writing a configuration file — both of which our own checklist fails, because the people this is built for cannot do either. It went unnoticed for six weeks because every test ran on a developer’s machine, where it had always been put there by hand. Now: a manager adds someone and gets a short activation code to pass on however they like. The person installs, opens Merlin, and Merlin asks them for it — they never have to work out what it is for, which was the whole point. Wrong, expired or already used each get their own reply with a way forward; none of them says “invalid”, because that is the moment somebody decides they have broken it. And the security review of the fix found a real hole in the fix. Great care had been taken that a manager could not choose what level of access a code granted — the option does not exist in three separate places. But a manager could point a replacement code at somebody else’s record, and the record carried the access. Demonstrated for real: a code was issued against the business owner and redeemed into full owner access. Closed twice over, and the lesson written down where the next person will read it: removing the option is not the same as removing the ability.

P4F4 reversal5 Aug

The safety warning we were proud of was the wrong warning

Merlin was about to start interrupting people before they pasted anything with a customer’s name in it. We had reported the absence of that warning as our sharpest finding of the week — five occasions across ten test sessions where the moment arrived and nothing was said. Keitha ruled that the silence was right and the rule was wrong. “That advice is sensible about a public chatbot and has nothing to do with what we’re selling. Our entire proposition is one sanctioned place with guardrails round it, which means putting the business’s information into the business’s own Claude is the point of the purchase, not the risk in it.” Her own most-used tool would have been forbidden by it. So the interruption is gone, and what survives is about which tool rather than which information — the company’s installation is sanctioned; a personal AI account on a home laptop is not. One rule remains the only one allowed to interrupt work that is going well: an answer containing a fact nobody supplied, caught before it is sent. The trap in undoing it, which is why it was swept before it was edited: two rules used almost identical words and pointed opposite ways. Removing the warning must not touch the separate rule that keeps a customer’s name out of what gets transmitted — the one strengthened the same day. And all ten of the trigger lines now record what they actually are: drafts, assembled from a document written over one weekend in June, signed off untested. Her instruction was to treat every one of them as provisional.

P6TASK-017b5 Aug

Merlin was forgetting people, and the reason it was built that way was never true

A person’s notes were filed under the folder their terminal happened to be open in. Start from anywhere else and Merlin had never met them — so it asked them to introduce themselves again, while the record of what they had achieved sat safely somewhere else. What they had done survived; who they were did not. Keitha made this the one thing to fix before any installation: “memory is what the product runs on… if it keys wrongly in a real install, Merlin appears to have forgotten the customer, and that’s the hardest kind of fault for somebody to forgive.” Notes are now filed under the person. And the reason they had been filed by folder turns out to have been false all along. Our own design document said that folder loads itself into every conversation automatically, which is why the person’s notes were put there. It does not, and never did for any of Merlin’s files. Warmth is the floor and never moves; reassurance is the dial. Stance transitions too: repeated struggle softens, impatience briskens, and anti-lock-in re-stances after one failed adjustment rather than two — two-to-trigger plus two-to-re-stance leaves someone stuck six turns, and a nervous learner has gone by four.

P1TASK-012 · 01331 Jul

Per-user memory + dial persistence ✓

profile.md, pathway.md, ledger.jsonl, outbox.jsonl, in two directories rather than one: recoverable state where auto-loading helps, the irreplaceable record where no consolidator walks. Each dial stores a value, a one-line reason and when it last moved — and the reason is held to a stricter test than surviving the next session: it has to survive being read by the person it is about. Write what someone did, never what they are.

P0TASK-01029 Jul

Phase 0 verification ✓ — all five properties hold

A forged token is refused, a learner token can’t read team rows, a prose payload is rejected, a duplicate event_id dedupes, and the outbox survives an offline flush. 36 vitest tests against the real deployed backend — mocks would pass equally well on a system that was wide open — plus 30 database-level assertions in tests/phase0-rls.sql, which covers what a test client cannot reach: the policies need a session with claims set, and a Merlin token is deliberately not a database credential. P0TASK-00929 Jul

Sentry on Edge Functions only ✓

Never the MCP server — that runs on the learner’s machine, where a stack trace is an egress path the payload validator never inspects. defaultIntegrations: false is mandatory rather than tuning: the Deno SDK doesn’t instrument Deno.serve, so on a reused runtime one learner’s context can surface attached to another learner’s error. There is deliberately no captureException and no free-form message — the only entry point takes a closed set of enum fields, each checked against a safe pattern, so “never interpolate a rejected value” is a property of the code rather than a rule to remember. Pinned to sentry@8.55.0; the unpinned URL the spec suggested would resolve to whatever is latest at deploy time.

P0TASK-00829 Jul

Passive session telemetry ✓

The signal that separates “nobody is using this” from “eight of ten seats were active this week” — and it costs the learner nothing, because it never asks them anything. Shipped as .mjs rather than .sh: Merlin has to run on Windows, and Node is already required, which also lets the hook reuse the server’s event-id code instead of reimplementing the hash in shell. SessionStart does no network I/O at all — 52ms. Session duration is recorded via a per-session marker so duration_minutes reaches the utilisation metric. cwd is discarded outright; the session id is hashed into the event id and never transmitted.

P0TASK-00729 Jul

Local outbox + merlin_emit_event ✓

Disk first, network later: emit measured at ~40ms and returns before any request is made, so an unreachable backend is invisible to the learner. event_id = sha256(learner_id | type | natural_key) — a random UUID would make every retry a new row, so utilisation and ROI would drift upward on exactly the flaky connections where someone retries most, and nothing would look wrong. natural_key is hashed and discarded, never transmitted, which is what lets the session hook key on a session id without it becoming telemetry. Compaction renames rather than locks, so two concurrent sessions sharing one outbox is safe.

P0TASK-00629 Jul

Ingest + the payload allow-list ✓

No secret key on this path. It connects over the database URL, drops to the authenticated role and sets the claims from the verified token, so RLS enforces rather than an if in the handler. Privilege drops mid-transaction on purpose — the revocation check runs first, because that function isn’t executable by authenticated. The allow-list contains no bare string type: every string derives from the same pattern the database CHECK enforces, so nothing can pass validation and then fail in Postgres. A structural scan runs before schema validation and rejects reflection, evidence, expressiveness_read at any depth, so smuggled content produces a named, alertable rejection. Each row inserts in its own savepoint — without that, one poison event would abort every batch forever and silently end a learner’s telemetry.

P0TASK-00529 Jul

Bundled MCP server ✓

stdio, and fail-soft by contract: missing, expired, malformed and corrupt identity are all states it reports calmly, never exceptions it throws, because a broken tool panel makes Julie think she broke something. The token never reaches a tool result or stderr. The client decodes claims without verifying — it has no key, by design — so that gate is surface reduction for TASK-047, never the permission check.

P0TASK-00429 Jul

Server-issued identity that can’t be forged ✓

HS256 tokens signed with a key held only as a project secret, so the plugin can present an identity but never invent one. The key id is derived from the key material, so rotation needs no bookkeeping. One entry point does signature, claims and revocation together — a caller cannot skip the revocation check — and a registry outage fails closed rather than falling through to “valid”. Failures are enum codes; no rejected value ever reaches a log. The registry stores only the token id, never the token, so a dump of it grants nothing. Verified: self-elevation to owner, impersonation, business switching, alg:none and unknown-key are all refused.

P0TASK-00329 Jul

RLS default-deny on every table ✓

Seven tables, 14 policies, and anon holds zero grants anywhere — there is no unauthenticated surface. Team reads require the token to claim manager/owner and the live row to agree, so demoting someone takes effect on the next request rather than at token expiry, and a stale elevated token stops working immediately. business_id is derived from the learners table, never read from the token: a team-scoped read must never take a caller-supplied business, and a claimed one is still caller-supplied. A manager cannot read rubric-level judgement at all — that is exactly the “individual fumbles” they are never shown.

P0TASK-00229 Jul

Schema — with the no-prose rule as a constraint ✓

Six tables per the PRD, no messages table, and exactly one documented free-text column (nominations.label, present only after an explicit human decision to share). The privacy invariant is a CHECK constraint, not an ingest convention: every payload string must be space-free and under 128 characters, so a sentence cannot be stored at all — a whitelist rather than a length heuristic, because “reject strings over N chars” would still let too_slow_again through as smuggled sentiment. events is append-only by trigger (UPDATE and DELETE raise even for the secret key), event_id must be a hash so a later change can’t regress to random UUIDs, and business_id is stamped from the learner rather than trusted from the request.
