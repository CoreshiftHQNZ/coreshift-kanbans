- **Scores that don't drift — and a command that proves it rather than claiming it** `M2` `method` `shipped` — The risk carried into this milestone was that two of the tools we surveyed produce audit scores that wander ±10 points on a site nobody touched, which would make every month-over-month claim we build on top fiction. So the scoring was designed backwards from that threat: enumerate every way an unchanged site could score differently twice, then close each one. The ones that mattered — the page set is decided from the sitemap and sorted the same way every time rather than by whichever page answers first; **no rule is allowed to look at today's date**, because a "content updated in the last 90 days" check makes an untouched site score lower every month and the report would call that a regression; a page we couldn't read is dropped from the calculation instead of counted as a failure; and the ruleset is stamped with a version, so if we ever change the weights the app refuses to draw a comparison across the change rather than letting a methodology tweak look like a client result. **Verified by:** `npm run audit -- --client storepro --verify` crawling the site twice and reporting identical results across all 23 checks, plus two separately stored runs both recording 91.50 / 93.44 / 45.45. Reasoning for every rule is written up in `docs/scoring.md`.

## 🔵 This Week

- **One sweep is not a measurement — needs a sampling design** `M5` `method` `risk` — The biggest thing M3 found, and it is a limitation. Two full sweeps of the same 15 prompts **minutes apart** disagreed on **5 of 15**: three where the AI Overview appeared or vanished entirely, one where a citation flipped, one where the rank moved from 2 to 4. This is the same threat the audit scoring was designed backwards from — a number that moves on its own makes every month-over-month claim fiction — except **the audit's drift sources were ours and could be closed, and this one is Google's and cannot be.** Until a sampling design exists (n sweeps over a window, reported as a rate with its sample size), no citation delta may be shown to a client. Belongs in M5 with predictions and controls, because the answer is a confidence interval, not a bug fix. ✅ **The M4 half shipped.** M4 stamps a mandatory `rationale` on every work item and that field becomes the report's "why" — so a work item derived from a single sweep would put a rationale on a coin flip, which is precisely what this product exists to prevent. A probe-sourced work item now carries its sample size and n<2 cannot be ranked — enforced by a check constraint, and three of Storepro's approved items are discounted to confidence 0.4 on n=2 because of it. The sampling *design* stays here, and when it raises n these items rise on their own with no re-tuning.
- **The other four engines** `M4` `blocked-on-ricky` — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Claude adapters are written, typechecked and waiting; each switches on the moment its key appears in the env. One engine is a data point, four is a panel — and ChatGPT is the one a client will ask about by name.
- **CITE port + per-client competitor cohorts** `M4` `method` — Now unblocked: DataForSEO is live and Backlinks is entitled (Storepro: **91 referring domains, 280 backlinks, rank 172**). The manual part still doesn't automate away — each client needs a locked 3–5 competitor cohort declared by an analyst, and until Storepro has one the panel correctly reports competitor presence as *not measured* rather than guessing.

- **The M3 "citation flipped" data point may be a detector artefact** `M5` `method` `risk` — Found while wiring the probe source into the plan. M3's two sweeps straddled a detector change: the first 10 answered runs are `2026-08-a` with an **empty** `engine_sources` array, every later run is `2026-08-b` with 8–14 structured sources. The AI-Overview-appeared-or-vanished changes (3 of the 5) are presence changes and stand regardless. But "one citation flipped" could be the detector rather than Google, and it cannot be settled by re-judging — `rejudge` correctly refuses those runs because re-reading prose alone would drop references the engine returned. **This does not overturn the M3 finding; it weakens one of its five data points.** A clean n≥3 sweep under one detector version settles it, and the M5 sampling design produces that anyway.
- **Effort estimates have never been calibrated** `M5` `method` — The plan's hours are declared constants in `server/plan/rank.ts`, labelled as estimates everywhere they appear. Nothing has checked them against how long the work actually took. `work_items.shipped_at` makes it answerable after two or three cycles — worth doing before hours are ever quoted to a client.

## 🅿️ Parking Lot

- **Work item owners** — `work_items.owner_id` exists and nothing sets it. With one specialist it isn't a real question yet; it becomes one at M7 alongside per-client assignment.
- **Capacity derived from the retainer** — `clients.monthly_retainer_nzd` is on file, but hours-per-dollar is a pricing decision and not the ranker's to invent. Capacity stays an explicit input until Ricky sets a rate.
- **Cross-source fix detection** — An AI-probe observation and an audit finding may share a fix. Deciding that automatically means guessing, and a wrong guess deletes real work from the month, so the merge only collapses declared overlaps. If a pattern emerges across clients it could be worth revisiting with a declared mapping rather than fuzzy matching.
- **Restoring the strict supersede constraint** — `0007` weakened "every superseded plan names its successor" to "a successor pointer may only appear on a superseded plan", because the strict pair was unsatisfiable in either order and Postgres cannot defer a CHECK. The forward guarantee now lives in `writePlan`, which is weaker. A single SQL function doing the whole supersede would put it back in the database.
- **Performance / Core Web Vitals as a fourth pillar** — Deliberately left out of the audit score in M2. Measuring it properly needs a rendering engine and real-user field data, and both would put a number that moves on its own into a client-facing score — the exact failure the scoring method was built to prevent. If we want it, it belongs in its own pillar with its own comparability rules.
- **Rendered-DOM comparison** — Right now we infer that a page needs JavaScript from what's missing in the raw HTML. Running a headless browser would measure the raw-versus-rendered gap directly and would be a genuinely better diagnosis. It stays out of the *score* even then: a headless browser is the least reproducible thing we could put in one.
- **Content production pipeline** — Briefs, drafts and publishing are a separate concern. Work items can reference them by URL until we know we need more.
- **CMS write adapters** — Applying changes directly to client sites (WordPress, Shopify, Webflow, git PR). Approval-gated, and not needed before M7.
- **Multi-agency tenancy** — Built for Growth Partners as a single agency. If Coreshift needs an isolated instance, that's a column plus RLS. Cheap to add later, premature now.
- **API cost accounting** — At 100 clients, per-client API spend becomes real and billable. Worth a table before we get there.
- **Local SEO / Google Business Profile** — Not in scope until a client needs it.
- **Calibration study** — After ~12 months we'll have change/prediction/outcome data across every client. That corpus is the moat: it lets us answer "do refreshes on position 8–15 pages actually beat control?" from our own evidence rather than quoting unsourced blog percentages.
