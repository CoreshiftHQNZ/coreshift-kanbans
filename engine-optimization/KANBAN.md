- **"Every page we didn't touch" is not a control — and it took real data to prove it** `M6` `finding` — The single most important thing found this milestone, and it would have shipped as a bug in any tool that reasoned about it instead of measuring. Over the four months before the change the untouched group grew **25%** while the targeted group sat **flat** — they are different kinds of page with different trajectories. So membership is *matched*, not merely complementary: one-to-one on the shape of the pre-period series (each page indexed to its own mean), baselines within 5×, no page used twice, and both sides needing a complete history across every month. **Then it checks itself and refuses**: under five pairs, one page carrying over 40% of a group, or more than ten points of pre-period divergence, and there is no prediction. ## 🅿️ Parking Lot

- **The prediction window assumes the work ships inside the approval month** — Anchored to `work_plans.approved_at`, because `work_items.shipped_at` is unknown when the prediction is written and waiting for it would mean choosing the window *after* the change, which is the one thing forbidden. If the work slips past August, October is no longer a clean month and the honest outcome is `confounded` rather than `failed`. The field exists, the check does not — it belongs with the verification writer in M7.
- **`predictions.confounders` has no writer** — `algorithm_updates` has existed since `0001` and nothing writes to it, so a core update landing inside a readback window will not flag itself. Until the ledger exists, confounders are a hand-typed array in M7. Google-owned sources only, verified entries only.
- **The readback does not mark an item shipped** — It reports; a specialist decides. "The audit says this check passes" and "this work is done" are different claims, and only the first is one an audit run can establish. Wiring `finished` straight into `work_items.status` would be the unenforced checklist arriving through yet another door — but there is a good case for a one-click *"the readback agrees, mark it shipped"* in the app once a specialist other than Ricky is using it.
- **`gsc_position` and the GA4 metrics are declared and unused** — `metric_kind` carries ten metrics and the mechanism table only ever points at `gsc_impressions` and `gsc_clicks`. Position is available per page and would be a better early indicator than impressions for several rules; it was left out because "lower is better" inverts the direction logic and nothing needed it yet.
- **The directive constraint has an UPDATE-shaped hole** — `0009` requires a complete directive on every item *inserted* into a plan, enforced by a `before insert` trigger. It cannot be an unconditional NOT NULL, because the approved M4 plan `62ff1c2c` has none and rewriting an approved plan's items to satisfy a constraint added afterwards would be editing history to make the present tidy. The consequence: an UPDATE could still blank a directive to `{}` and the CHECK would allow it, because the CHECK has to allow the pre-M5 rows to exist at all. Nothing writes such an update today. Closable when no unapproved pre-M5 items remain — same shape as the `0007` supersede weakening, and parked next to it.
- **An action does not check its prerequisite's targets overlap** — The plan orders `aeo.faq_schema` behind `geo.structured_data`, and the FAQ item's steps tell the specialist to check each page has visible Q&A first. Nothing verifies the two items' *page sets* actually intersect. On Storepro they largely do; on a client where they did not, the ordering would be conservative rather than wrong, which is the safe direction to be imprecise in. Worth doing when a second client makes it observable.
- **`obs.stale_content` targets could be recovered by storing sitemap lastmod per page** — It is the one rule whose full affected list cannot be re-derived, because `lastmod` is sitemap data and page snapshots hold page data. One column on `page_snapshots` fixes it at the next audit run. Not worth a migration on its own; fold it into whatever next touches the crawl.
- **Work item owners** — `work_items.owner_id` exists and nothing sets it. With one specialist it isn't a real question yet; it becomes one at M7 alongside per-client assignment.
- **Capacity derived from the retainer** — `clients.monthly_retainer_nzd` is on file, but hours-per-dollar is a pricing decision and not the ranker's to invent. Capacity stays an explicit input until Ricky sets a rate.
- **Cross-source fix detection** — An AI-probe observation and an audit finding may share a fix. Deciding that automatically means guessing, and a wrong guess deletes real work from the month, so the merge only collapses declared overlaps. If a pattern emerges across clients it could be worth revisiting with a declared mapping rather than fuzzy matching.
- **Restoring the strict supersede constraint** — `0007` weakened "every superseded plan names its successor" to "a successor pointer may only appear on a superseded plan", because the strict pair was unsatisfiable in either order and Postgres cannot defer a CHECK. The forward guarantee now lives in `writePlan`, which is weaker. A single SQL function doing the whole supersede would put it back in the database.
- **Performance / Core Web Vitals as a fourth pillar** — Deliberately left out of the audit score in M2. Measuring it properly needs a rendering engine and real-user field data, and both would put a number that moves on its own into a client-facing score — the exact failure the scoring method was built to prevent. If we want it, it belongs in its own pillar with its own comparability rules.
- **Rendered-DOM comparison** — Right now we infer that a page needs JavaScript from what's missing in the raw HTML. Running a headless browser would measure the raw-versus-rendered gap directly and would be a genuinely better diagnosis. It stays out of the *score* even then: a headless browser is the least reproducible thing we could put in one.
- **Content production pipeline** — Briefs, drafts and publishing are a separate concern. Work items can reference them by URL until we know we need more.
- **CMS write adapters** — Applying changes directly to client sites (WordPress, Shopify, Webflow, git PR). Approval-gated, and not needed before M7.
- **Multi-agency tenancy** — Built for Growth Partners as a single agency. If Coreshift needs an isolated instance, that's a column plus RLS. Cheap to add later, premature now.
- **API cost accounting** — At 100 clients, per-client API spend becomes real and billable. Worth a table before we get there.
- **Local SEO / Google Business Profile** — Not in scope until a client needs it.
- **Calibration study** — After ~12 months we'll have change/prediction/outcome data across every client. That corpus is the moat: it lets us answer "do refreshes on position 8–15 pages actually beat control?" from our own evidence rather than quoting unsourced blog percentages.
