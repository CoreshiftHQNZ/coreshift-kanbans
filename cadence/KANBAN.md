## ✅ Done

- **📐 M1 — the idea, pressure-tested and reframed before a line of code** `M1` `planning` `shipped` — Ran the full ideation workflow. **Verdict: Strong, 25/30** — but the framing had to change to get there. The brief was "end-to-end GST filing"; validation established that **Xero NZ already computes the GST return and already files it to IRD**, so building that would rebuild something Colab gets free. The labour is everything that must be true *before* anyone presses the button — miscoded lines, wrong tax rates, unreconciled bank items, period-boundary strays, unexplained movement, records the client hasn't sent — and because the Google Sheet holding that checklist proves nothing, **the reviewer re-derives every return from scratch. Colab pays for the review twice.** So the product is the exception sweep + traceable workpaper + named sign-off, and filing is a feature on the end. **Three fatal flaws ranked with fast tests**, the sharpest being that the core assumption is still unverified: if the hours actually go into chasing clients rather than checking coding, the engine automates the cheap half and headcount doesn't move — which is why M2 and M3 contain no software. **Competition mapped honestly**, including the one that matters: Xero's own rebuilt Workpapers with a GST worksheet landing this year, bundled, from a vendor with far more resource than us. Six documents written: `product-idea.md`, `validation-report.md`, `VISION.md` (validated field-by-field against the skill's template by script), `product-vision.md`, `prd.md` (10 tables, 4 database-level integrity triggers, 22 endpoints, 25 functional requirements, 8 open questions each with a recommendation), and `product-roadmap.md`. **Two premises are labelled unverified throughout rather than quietly assumed:** the GST101A box map is *not* implemented from memory — it gets verified against IRD's live form first — and the 11-rule exception catalogue is explicitly a **hypothesis** to be rewritten from Colab's actual sheet in M2.

- **🗄️ The feasibility spike, harvested and archived** `M1` `decision` `shipped` — `core-ricky/cadence` was **a prototype built to prove a function was possible, not a foundation** — 43 commits in one week (2026-06-15 → 06-22), then dormant two months. Nothing runs in parallel, so there's no half-finished port to strand. Renamed to **`cadence-spike`** and archived read-only, with a superseded banner pointing here; the *name* Cadence was kept for the product because a good name shouldn't go in the bin with the code. **Harvested as knowledge, not code:** the confidence rule (`consistent` / `conflicted` / `rare`), the payee-key normalisation, the proven result (8 of 26 lines auto-reconciled correctly, Woolworths/Kmart correctly deferred as conflicted), the schema shape as reference, and the operational lessons — truth-sync, the progress watchdog, payee grouping, Railway's `npm ci` optional-dep failure, the middleware relative-`Location` 500. **🔴 One honest cost of the clean break:** the exception engine's baseline was going to be the spike's live `coding_profiles`. - **📏 The GST101A box map, verified from IRD's own form — and four things the PRD didn't anticipate** `M2` `compliance` `shipped` — TASK-006 done, and the 🔴 "do not implement from memory" warning is discharged the right way: the forms were downloaded from IRD and text-extracted, then cross-checked against **IR375 (March 2026)**, the current edition of IRD's own guide. **The PRD's indicative numbering was correct** — Boxes 1–4 identification, 5–15 the return, with every derivation confirmed verbatim (`7 = 5−6`, `8 = 7×3÷23`, `10 = 8+9`, `12 = 11×3÷23`, `14 = 12+13`, `15 = |10−14|`). Four findings change the build: **(a) Boxes 9 and 13 are not ledger-derivable at all** — both read *"from your calculation sheet"*, i.e. manual GST adjustments — so they default to `traceable=false`, **and Boxes 10, 14 and 15 inherit non-traceability whenever either is non-zero.** The lower half of the return is only as traceable as its weakest input, and the engine has to propagate that. **(b) Imported goods are excluded from Box 11** and claimed as a Box 13 credit adjustment (IR375 p.33: *"Do not include imported goods under purchases on your GST return"*) — that is a real exception rule with a hard IRD citation and a likely error in a Xero-coded ledger, banked as `IMPORT_IN_BOX_11`. **(c) Box 15 is a magnitude plus a direction**, `refund`/`to_pay`/`nil` — a signed number can't render the form and silently loses the nil case. **(d) GST103B Part 1 is byte-for-byte the GST101A return**, so `return_type` decides whether Parts 2–3 apply, not which box map to use — **one engine serves both**, a real simplification to M6. Resolves `prd.md § 14 Q4`. Also recorded: **IR372 is a dead end** — the only published version is August 2010 and IRD files it under *"before 1 April 2011"*; IR375 Part 4 is the live authority on adjustments.

- **📊 The coding-profile baseline is feasible — measured, not hoped** `M2` `M5` `data` `shipped` — The honest cost of the clean break was that M5's baseline no longer came free, and it was unknown whether Colab's ledgers could even support learning one. **They can.** Measured across **277,595 real transaction lines from 139 client ledgers**: **61.9% of lines sit under a payee with a single dominant historical coding, and only 8.2% have no baseline at all.** 129 of 139 clients (93%) have five or more consistent payees; **none have zero**; median 37 per client. 🔴 **The trap worth recording: weighted by *payee key* the consistent share looks like 26.5%** — because keys are dominated by a long tail of one-off suppliers. That understates real coverage by more than 2×, and quoting it would have made M5 look far weaker than it is. So `prd.md` FR-010's *"a client with no profile cannot be swept"* turns out to exclude essentially nobody. 🔴 **But the provenance is the finding that matters more:** every one of the 147 client records carries a `manual-*` tenant id, and `xero_connections` and `xero_tokens` are **both empty** — **the spike never made a single Xero API connection.** The data came in through a browser extract. That means field shapes will differ after a real API pull (`tax_type` as a proper enum rather than the UI label `"GST"`), the numbers must be re-measured in M7 rather than carried forward — and, per the Xero email above, the extract route itself is now in question. The 29.9% of lines under `conflicted` payees isn't a failure either; it's where the precision target gets won or lost.

- **🔎 Colab's GST workbook, found and read — and the product's core claim is now evidenced, not argued** `M2` `research` `shipped` — A June 2026 export of `GST Client List.xlsx` was already sitting on this machine, so M2 got a head start on two blocked tasks without waiting for anyone. Nine tabs. 🔴 **The headline is a negative result that strengthens the case: there is no per-return checklist anywhere in it.** M1's thesis was that the sheet's tick *proves nothing*; the reality is thinner still — the `Reviewed` column is a **bare date**, no reviewer name, no record of what was checked, nothing to drill into, and free-text `Notes` appears on **6 of 167 rows**. The reviewer re-deriving every return isn't a habit to be trained out of them; **it's the only rational response to a sign-off with no evidence attached.** That claim is now sourced from the artefact. **Three things came out free.** (1) **`GST Rec`** is a genuine workpaper, referenced **D.1 / D.2**: it ties the GST control account in the balance sheet to the returns filed, via arrears, timing differences on AR/AP for payments-basis clients, and an errors-and-adjustments block — ending in a **`Difference` that must be zero**. Mechanically computable, high-value, and **completely absent from `prd.md` FR-011's hypothesised catalogue.** (2) Colab's own formula is **`ROUND(gross * 3/23, 2)`** — the exact fraction verified from IRD's form, which **corroborates the box map and settles the rounding convention** rather than leaving it to preference. (3) A 21-rule supplier-coding sheet, hand-written by a Colab person, flagging each rule `auto` or **`needs attention`** — and the `needs attention` set is Bunnings/Supercheap, Woolworths/New World/Pak'nSave, Warehouse/Kmart/Briscoes/Temu, Chemist Warehouse, Afterpay. **The spike independently deferred Woolworths and Kmart as `conflicted`. The machine and the human agree on the hard cases** — free validation of the confidence rule from the side that matters. Also banked: a hand-written **entertainment threshold at $57.50** (50% GST-deductible in NZ, so miscoding it produces a wrong return — ⚠️ verify the figure against IRD first), a payee **suppression** rule the catalogue has no concept for yet, and `No bank feeds` as the preparer's most-repeated real-world problem. ⚠️ **It's a June export, not the live sheet** — counts drift (167 vs 158) — so every figure is provisional.

## 🅿️ Parking Lot

- **Bank reconciliation auto-posting** `parked` — The spike's whole function, and *not* needed for GST. GST needs the learned coding profile as a baseline; it does not need proposals, auto-posting or the reconcile loop. Real value and a plausible second module later — but carrying it now would double the build for no GST benefit. The spike proved it works (8/26 auto-reconciled correctly); that knowledge is banked.
- **Billing / Stripe** `parked` — The spike anticipated it; this product explicitly doesn't. The retainer is invoiced directly, so there are no payment rails, no billing UI and no PCI surface. Revisit only if Cadence is ever sold self-serve to multiple practices.
- **Client chase portal** `parked` `contingent` — Chasing clients for missing records may be a real share of the cycle. **If M2's timing shows chasing dominates, this stops being parked and becomes the pivot** — a different, cheaper product than the one currently planned.
- **Writing corrections back into Xero** `parked` — The preparer's natural next action after seeing a miscoded line, and bouncing to Xero is real friction. Deferred because writing to a client's ledger is the highest-consequence action available and needs trust the product hasn't earned. Reconsider after M8, behind explicit per-action confirmation.
- **Complex-basis and adjustment-heavy clients** `parked` `scope` — Payments and hybrid basis, private-use and entertainment adjustments, imported goods, zero-rated land, secondhand goods, bad debts, GST103 provisional-tax coupling. Each is a distinct ruleset. Declined **by design and in writing** rather than discovered as failures mid-cycle — which Coreshift can only do because it owns the asset. Unlock per category, ranked by how many client files each actually serves.
- **Multi-practice tenancy and self-serve onboarding** `parked` — `firm_id` is present throughout from the start, so this is small later; designing for practices we haven't met is speculative now.
- **CSV / data export** `parked` `security` — `gst_transactions` holds a client's complete financial detail, so an export path is a disclosure path. The workpaper PDF is the only artefact that leaves the system. Only on explicit request, with an access-controlled design.
- **Automated filing without a human signature** `never` — Not a scheduling decision. A named reviewer signs every return, always; filing something nobody looked at inverts the product's entire liability profile.
