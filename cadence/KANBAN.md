- **📏 The GST101A box map, verified from IRD's own form — and four things the PRD didn't anticipate** `M2` `compliance` `shipped` — TASK-006 done, and the 🔴 "do not implement from memory" warning is discharged the right way: the forms were downloaded from IRD and text-extracted, then cross-checked against **IR375 (March 2026)**, the current edition of IRD's own guide. **The PRD's indicative numbering was correct** — Boxes 1–4 identification, 5–15 the return, with every derivation confirmed verbatim (`7 = 5−6`, `8 = 7×3÷23`, `10 = 8+9`, `12 = 11×3÷23`, `14 = 12+13`, `15 = |10−14|`). Four findings change the build: **(a) Boxes 9 and 13 are not ledger-derivable at all** — both read *"from your calculation sheet"*, i.e. manual GST adjustments — so they default to `traceable=false`, **and Boxes 10, 14 and 15 inherit non-traceability whenever either is non-zero.** The lower half of the return is only as traceable as its weakest input, and the engine has to propagate that. **(b) Imported goods are excluded from Box 11** and claimed as a Box 13 credit adjustment (IR375 p.33: *"Do not include imported goods under purchases on your GST return"*) — that is a real exception rule with a hard IRD citation and a likely error in a Xero-coded ledger, banked as `IMPORT_IN_BOX_11`. **(c) Box 15 is a magnitude plus a direction**, `refund`/`to_pay`/`nil` — a signed number can't render the form and silently loses the nil case. **(d) GST103B Part 1 is byte-for-byte the GST101A return**, so `return_type` decides whether Parts 2–3 apply, not which box map to use — **one engine serves both**, a real simplification to M6. Resolves `prd.md § 14 Q4`. Also recorded: **IR372 is a dead end** — the only published version is August 2010 and IRD files it under *"before 1 April 2011"*; IR375 Part 4 is the live authority on adjustments.
