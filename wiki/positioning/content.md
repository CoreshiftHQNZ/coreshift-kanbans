# Positioning

Who Coreshift is for, and how we speak to them. This is the single source of truth for our market posture and voice — every design and copy decision should be testable against it.

---

## What Coreshift is

Coreshift HQ is a **single source of truth for business leaders on how to use AI.**

- **Platform- and model-agnostic** — not tied to OpenAI / Anthropic / Google / any vendor.
- **Alongside the leader, not above them** — advisory posture, not vendor-y.
- **The antidote to AI noise** — the market is loud, hype-saturated, and confusing. Coreshift cuts through it with calm authority.

> The test for any decision: **does this feel like signal, or like more noise?** Avoid hype language, dense jargon, AI-buzzword bingo, and startup-loud aesthetics. Favour restraint, whitespace, plain language, and grounded claims.

---

## Who we're speaking to

**Primary persona:** the GM, MD, or CEO of a **$10m–$50m revenue business.**

Their emotional state on arrival:

- Their LinkedIn feed is saturated with AI consultants, hot takes, and fear-driven "you're falling behind" content.
- **Fear and frustration are building** — not yet resolved, actively compounding.
- They're cycling through: *"Should we hire someone? Which tool? Are we already too late? Is this real or hype?"*

How to speak to them:

- Acknowledge the noise they're drowning in **before** offering anything.
- Avoid any phrase that could appear in the feed they're exhausted by — "transform your business with AI," "don't get left behind," "the AI revolution."
- Tone = the **calm advisor across the desk**, not the consultant on stage.
- Assume they are **intelligent and accomplished, just overwhelmed.** Never condescend or "educate."

---

## Design vision

Inspiration: the **2013 Apple Mac Pro product page** — scroll-driven storytelling, progressive disclosure, cinematic pacing. Not generic "Apple minimalism," but that era's scroll-reveal choreography.

Adapted for Coreshift, the feel:

- **Calm, peaceful, spacious** — generous whitespace, long vertical rhythm, no density.
- **Clarity, peace, trust** — the three emotional outcomes we're after.
- **Scroll-as-narrative** — one idea at a time, not a dashboard of everything at once.
- **Drawn in, not pitched at** — cinematic reveal over marketing-site patterns.

Practical guidance: full-viewport sections with one focal idea each; resist side-by-side card/feature/logo walls; let typography breathe; **when in doubt, cut a section rather than compress it.**

---

## The homepage narrative arc

The homepage is a scroll-revealed narrative — one idea per beat, in this order. The pacing **is** the product; resist combining beats "for efficiency."

1. **Quiet the noise** *(opening)* — the headline lands against a cloud of real-feeling LinkedIn AI-hype fragments. The site arrives as an intervention on the reality the visitor already lives in.
2. **Just breathe** — the noise fades and blurs; the invitation emerges. The breath is the relief they earn by staying — the moment the nervous system unclenches.
3. **Recognition** — a single hard-hitting line that makes them think *"this is EXACTLY me."* Currently: *"You're not afraid of AI. You're afraid of being sold something that won't work."*
4. **Reassurance** — "it's not too late, you're not missing out." Lower the cortisol further.
5. **Reframe** — AI is **a way of thinking, not a choice of technology.** The pivotal insight.
6. **Proof of method** — there is a proven method to what we do. Method over product pitch.
7. **Invitation** — "let's chat." A single, quiet door — not a CTA button farm.

> Leading with the *pain* (the noise) lands harder than leading with the invitation. The breath then becomes *earned relief* rather than an abstract opener. Never skip from pain straight to offer — the reassurance and reframe beats are load-bearing.

**Palette / atmosphere:** light and airy — **dawn, open sky, breath.** First light, high altitude, horizon visible, room to think. Not dark and reverent; not warm and earthy.
